Most college courses don't grade by a simple average. The syllabus splits your grade into categories (homework, quizzes, exams) and gives each one a weight. A 95 on homework and a 70 on the midterm don't average to 82.5 if the midterm counts three times as much.

Here's how to do the math yourself, step by step.

## Step 1: find the weights in your syllabus

Look for a section called "Grading," "Assessment" or "Course grade." It usually looks like this:

| Category | Weight |
|---|---|
| Homework | 20% |
| Quizzes | 15% |
| Midterm | 25% |
| Final exam | 40% |

The weights should add up to 100%. If they don't, check for a participation or lab category you missed.

Some courses skip weights entirely and grade by **total points**: every assignment is worth some number of points and your grade is points earned divided by points possible. If your syllabus lists points instead of percentages, jump to [courses graded by points](#courses-graded-by-points).

## Step 2: work out each category's average

Within a category, add up the points you earned and divide by the points possible. Don't average the percentages unless your syllabus says to.

Say your quizzes were 9 out of 10 and 40 out of 50:

- **Points:** (9 + 40) ÷ (10 + 50) = 49 ÷ 60 = **81.67%**
- **Averaging percentages:** (90% + 80%) ÷ 2 = 85%

Those aren't the same. The bigger quiz counts for more, which is how most gradebooks (including Canvas) work by default.

### If the lowest score is dropped

Many syllabi drop your lowest quiz or homework. Take it out before you add up. Quizzes of 6, 9, 8 and 10 out of 10 come to 33 ÷ 40 = 82.5%. Drop the 6 and it's 27 ÷ 30 = **90%**.

## Step 3: multiply by the weights and add

Suppose your category averages so far are:

| Category | Weight | Your average |
|---|---|---|
| Homework | 20% | 92% |
| Quizzes | 15% | 84% |
| Midterm | 25% | 78% |
| Final exam | 40% | not taken yet |

Multiply each average by its weight:

- Homework: 0.20 × 92 = 18.4
- Quizzes: 0.15 × 84 = 12.6
- Midterm: 0.25 × 78 = 19.5

That's 50.5 points out of the 60% of the course you've been graded on so far.

**Your current grade is 50.5 ÷ 0.60 = 84.17%.** You divide by 0.60, not 1.00, because the final hasn't happened yet. Leaving it in would treat it as a zero and show you a grade of 50.5%, which is the most common reason students panic over a grade that's actually fine.

## Step 4: what do you need on the final?

Pick the course grade you want, subtract what you've already earned, and divide by the final's weight:

> needed on final = (target grade − points earned so far) ÷ final's weight

With the numbers above:

- For an **85%**: (85 − 50.5) ÷ 0.40 = **86.25%** on the final
- For a **90%**: (90 − 50.5) ÷ 0.40 = **98.75%** on the final
- For an **80%**: (80 − 50.5) ÷ 0.40 = **73.75%** on the final

If the answer is over 100%, that grade is out of reach unless there's extra credit or a curve. That's useful to know early, before you plan your study time around it.

## Courses graded by points

When there are no weights, your grade is just:

> grade = total points earned ÷ total points possible

If you've earned 412 points out of 470 so far, you're at 412 ÷ 470 = **87.66%**. To see what the final needs to be, add its points to both sides: with a 200-point final and a 90% target, you need 0.90 × 670 = 603 points in total, so 603 − 412 = 191 out of 200 on the final.

## Three mistakes that throw the math off

1. **Counting missing work as zero, or ignoring real zeros.** Ungraded work shouldn't count yet; a missed assignment that's been graded as a zero should.
2. **Averaging category percentages without the weights.** A 95, 85 and 70 average to 83.3, but with weights of 20%, 15% and 65% the grade is 77.25%.
3. **Trusting the Canvas total without checking.** If your instructor hasn't set up weighted groups in Canvas, or keeps some scores in Gradescope or a spreadsheet, the number Canvas shows can be off from the one in the syllabus.
